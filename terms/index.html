Last updated September 13, 2026 · VibeShield is MIT-licensed open source — this document explains the license, not a product you buy.

plain-language note — VibeShield is free, MIT-licensed software. You can use it on anything, including closed commercial code. In exchange, the tool makes no promises it cannot keep — and neither do we. The binding text is the MIT license; this page is that text with the Latin removed.

1.Acceptance

VibeShield — the scanner binary, the GitHub Action, the pre-commit hook, the npm launcher, and every rules pack in this repository — is free, open-source software released under the MIT License. There is no contract to sign and no terms to click through to run it. By using the software you are agreeing that this page accurately describes what it does: a static analyzer that reads your files, pattern-matches them against public rules, and prints what it finds.

The legal license text is the MIT license itself, reproduced at the bottom of this page and in the LICENSE file in the repository. Everything above the license line on this page is a plain-language guide to it — helpful, but not the operative text.

2.What the MIT license gives you

You may use VibeShield on any codebase — personal, commercial, public, or private — at no cost and without attribution obligations. You may modify it, fork it, recompile it with your own rules, and ship the result inside your company. You may quote its output in your own documentation or blog. The only substantive condition is that redistributed copies of the software itself keep the copyright and license notice.

Your findings are yours. Scan output — JSON, SARIF, PR comments, badge images — carries no license restrictions from us; publish it, delete it, or keep it under NDA as your situation requires.

3.No warranty — stated plainly

The software is provided "as is", without warranty of any kind, express or implied, including merchantability and fitness for a particular purpose. In practice this means a few concrete things, none of them hidden:

  • A clean scan is not a guarantee of security. The ruleset covers ~120 known AI-code failure modes across 8 languages; it does not cover the ones nobody has catalogued yet, and it is not a substitute for a human who reads the diff.
  • The maintainers are not liable for losses arising from use of the tool — including the costs of a missed finding or a false-positive delay. This is the standard MIT limitation; we are stating it, not burying it.
  • Release tags, rule-pack versions, and the Action are provided without an SLA. Support is community-first: GitHub issues, read by the people who write the rules.

4.What we ask you to do

The tool is only honest if its users are, so three asks:

  • Treat findings as advice, not verdicts. Every finding carries a why and a suggested fix; a human who understands the code makes the accept/dismiss call. The dismiss flow writes an auditable reason precisely so the judgement is yours and provably yours later.
  • Do not use the name, the shield logo, or the badge to imply endorsement by the maintainers or by any vendor VibeShield happens to scan. The badge states a scan result, not a relationship.
  • If you redistribute a modified scanner, make clear it is a fork. Security tooling that silently diverges from its upstream helps nobody.
